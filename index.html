Constellation Quest

Walk the sky. Name the stars. Open the gates.

Every gate is a puzzle about the 88 constellations the IAU recognizes. Name them from their shapes, draw them star by star, place them in the right season, and learn who shares a border with whom. Questions are drawn fresh every time you enter a world or replay a gate, so each run is different. Solve a gate and its constellation lights up above you.

    Practice your own constellations

    Type one constellation per line. Use a name like Orion or the IAU abbreviation like UMa. Pick the kinds of gates you want, and the game builds a path from your list (up to 18 gates).

    How to play
    • Walk: W A S D or the arrow keys. On a tablet or phone, use the joystick.
    • Solve a gate: walk up to it and press E (or tap Solve). A gate stays shut until you solve the one before it.
    • Answer buttons: press 1 to 9 to pick, Enter to check, Esc to close.
    • Draw gates: tap or click one star, then another, to draw a line between them. Drag from star to star works too. Tap the same two stars again to erase a line. Keyboard: Tab to a star, Enter to pick it.
    • Memory gates: the figure flashes for a few seconds, then disappears. Draw it from memory.
    • Seasons mean the evening sky (about 9 pm) as seen from the mid-northern United States.
    • Neighbors are constellations that share a border line on the official IAU map. Touching at a single corner does not count.
    • Fresh questions: every time you enter a world, its six gates get new constellations and new wrong answers. Replaying a gate you already opened also gives a new question. Stars are the best you have earned at each gate position.
    • New questions every run: each world draws a fresh set of six gates from all 88 constellations when you start it. Leave and come back and you resume the same run. Finish a world and replay it for a new set. Your best run is kept.
    • Stars: 3 stars if you get it right with no hints and no misses. 2 stars for one or two hints or misses. 1 star for more than that, or if a hint reveals the answer.
    Settings
    No accounts, no tracking. Progress is saved only on this device. Star positions and constellation borders come from the open d3-celestial data set (IAU boundaries). 3D graphics by Three.js (MIT license).
